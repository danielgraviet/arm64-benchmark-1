## Arm64 data point: guest wall clock = template age behind, and a knock-on latency effect

Measured on the HQ Vera cell on 2026-10-14. Runner from `f0611fa13` (where `LoadSnapshot` omits `clock_realtime` on arm64), stock Firecracker v1.16.1.

I ran `cat /proc/uptime; date +%s` in three fresh clones of one alpine template, created 2 s apart:

| clone | host time | guest uptime | guest wall clock |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1791991807 | 1.15 s | 1791981495 |
| 1 | 1791991809 | 1.16 s | 1791981495 |
| 2 | 1791991812 | 1.73 s | 1791981495 |

- **Wall clock:** every clone starts at the template's snapshot time, **~2 h 52 min behind the host**. That is the gap this issue describes, here on arm64 with no nested virtualization involved.
- **Monotonic clock:** it also resumes at the snapshot instant; it does not jump. This matters beyond wall time. The daemon's resume-triggered gratuitous ARP (`garp.rs`, `RESUME_JUMP`) relies on the monotonic clock jumping, so it never fires on arm64. That is one of the causes of a fixed ~580 ms first-connection stall in ~25% of clones: #3265.

Whatever this issue lands on for wall time, the daemon's resume detection should not depend on a monotonic jump.
